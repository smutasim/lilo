Your limits

Limits decide when a moment counts: when the Sun counts as visible and when Earth counts as in view. They apply in every view. The defaults only ask that some of the Sun and the centre of Earth are in view.

Communication

    Method, uncertainty and sources

    Nothing on this page is flight-grade. The page gives no rating and assumes no lander requirement: it reports quantities against your own limits and goals.

    Sun and Earth

    JPL DE440 through SPICE, apparent (light time and stellar aberration), in the Moon's mean Earth / polar axis frame (MOON_ME), precomputed every 2 h and interpolated. Checked against the JPL Horizons System at 4 sites and 6 epochs in 2027: the largest difference in azimuth or elevation is 0.00002° (0.0029° on the fallback path). Outside the precomputed span the page falls back to astronomy-engine and says so. The Sun and Earth are finite disks; "sunlit" means any part of the Sun is visible unless your limits say otherwise. No atmosphere.

    Terrain

    LOLA elevation models (5 m site tiles and 20 m polar mosaic from NASA GSFC PGDA, 240 m grid from PDS) turned into 3D horizon masks with curvature. Sites north of 60°S have no terrain and use a smooth sphere. The sky view draws the measured skyline; the ground below it is a flat tone, not a rendering of the surface.

    Imagery and 3D terrain (display only)

    The south-pole maps can show the LROC Wide Angle Camera south-pole summer mosaic (WAC_ROI_SOUTH_SUMMER_100M, Speyerer et al. 2020; images of 21 September to 23 October 2010, 643 nm), read from the PDS LROC node in its own polar stereographic projection, the projection of the LOLA grids. Against LOLA relief shaded with the mosaic's noon Sun, the image correlates at 0.884 with the best match at zero shift, so it is registered to within one 240 m pixel. Its shadows are those of 2010, not of the dial time.

    The 3D view draws a square of LOLA heights around a listed site south of 60°S (24 km at 100 m from the 20 m mosaic; 29 km at 240 m north of 80.6°S), true to scale with the Moon's curvature. Its "Sunlight now" surface marks ground that sees the centre of the Sun at the dial time, from the terrain inside the square and the site's own skyline beyond it. No number on the page comes from the 3D view or the imagery.

    Communication

    The direct link needs Earth's centre above the local LOLA skyline by your margin. If you require a ground antenna, a ticked one (the three DSN 70 m antennas, from DSN 810-005 Module 301, or your own) must have the Moon above its elevation limit, sampled every 30 min. A relay satellite adds a second path: it must clear the site's skyline by its own margin, the line from it to Earth's centre must pass outside the 1737.4 km sphere, and, if an antenna is required, an antenna must see the satellite. Satellites come from your state vector at an epoch (moved as a two-body orbit about the Moon, GM 4902.800118 km³/s² from DE440) or a JPL Horizons vector table centred on the Moon; ICRF positions are turned into the Moon's body-fixed frame with the IAU rotation model. Links are geometric: no link budget, antenna pattern or pointing.

    Irradiance

    The IAU 2015 nominal total solar irradiance, 1361 W/m² at 1 au, scaled by the Sun's distance and the visible fraction of its disk, on a surface that faces the Sun. No limb darkening, no array model.

    Uncertainty

    Where PGDA publishes error clones of the 5 m elevation model (Barker et al. 2021), headline numbers are recomputed on 16 of them and the page shows the lowest and highest result. That range covers elevation-model error only and is not a confidence interval. Everywhere else the terrain error is not quantified, and the page says so.

    Not modelled

    • Electrical powerIrradiance on a Sun-facing surface (W/m²), not watts: no array area, efficiency, pointing loss or limb darkening.
    • Relay orbits beyond two-body, and navigationRelay satellites are yours to enter: a state vector moves with the Moon's GM only (no Earth or Sun pull, no gravity field), or a pasted Horizons table is interpolated inside its span. No GNSS geometry, no satellite-to-ground pass scheduling.
    • Antenna patterns and link budgetLinks are geometric.
    • Thermal limits and lander-scale hazardsSlope is reported from LOLA at 10 m and 40 m baselines only. Rocks and craters below the grid spacing are not in the data.
    • Terrain away from the south poleSites north of 60°S use a smooth sphere.
