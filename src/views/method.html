Method and sources

Nothing here is flight-grade. No ratings: quantities against your own constraints and goals.
SubjectModelSource and check
Sun and Earth Apparent positions (light time and stellar aberration) from the Moon, in the Moon's mean Earth / polar axis frame (MOON_ME), precomputed every 2 h and interpolated. Finite disks; no atmosphere. "Sunlit" means any part of the Sun is visible unless the constraint says otherwise. Outside the precomputed span the page falls back to astronomy-engine and says so. JPL DE440 through SPICE. Against the JPL Horizons System at 4 sites and 6 epochs in 2027 the largest difference in azimuth or elevation is 0.00002° (0.0029° on the fallback path).
Terrain LOLA elevation models turned into 3D horizon masks with curvature, 1,440 azimuth bins, observer 2 m or 10 m above the ground. Sites north of 60°S have no terrain and use a smooth sphere. 5 m site tiles and 20 m polar mosaic from NASA GSFC PGDA (Barker et al. 2021), 240 m grid from PDS (LRO-L-LOLA-4-GDR-V1.0; Smith et al.). Average illumination compared with Mazarico et al. 2011 and the PGDA products (docs/validation.md).
Terrain uncertainty Where PGDA publishes error clones of the 5 m model, the headline numbers are recomputed on 16 of them and the lowest and highest results are shown. The range covers elevation-model error only and is not a confidence interval. Elsewhere terrain error is not quantified, and the page says so. Barker et al. 2021 clone elevation models.
Irradiance 1361 W/m² at 1 au, scaled by the Sun's distance and the visible fraction of its disk, on a surface that faces the Sun. No limb darkening, no array model: W/m², not watts. IAU 2015 Resolution B3 nominal total solar irradiance.
Link to Earth Direct: Earth's centre above the local skyline by the margin. With "ground stations" ticked, a ticked station must also have the Moon above its elevation limit (sampled every 30 min). Relay: a satellite marked as a relay must clear the site's skyline by its own margin, and the line from it to Earth's centre must pass outside the 1737.4 km sphere. Geometric only: no link budget, antenna pattern or pointing. DSN 70 m antenna coordinates and the 10.0° transmit limit: DSN 810-005, Module 301, Rev. N.
Satellite orbits
Satellite passes Rise and set against the LOLA skyline, scanned at 1/90 of the orbit period and bisected to 1 s. ICRF positions are turned into the Moon's body-fixed frame with the IAU rotation model. Link statistics sample a relay at the series step (10 min on the timeline, 1 h in the landing-date table and the finder), so passes shorter than that are under-sampled in the percentages; the timeline row and the rise and set times are exact. Two-body and Horizons-table paths checked against Horizons LRO tables (docs/validation.md, fifth pass).
Imagery and 3D terrain Display only: no number comes from them. South-pole maps show the LROC WAC south-pole summer mosaic (images of 21 September to 23 October 2010; its shadows are those dates'). The 3D view draws a 24 km square of LOLA heights at 100 m (29 km at 240 m north of 80.6°S), true to scale with curvature. WAC_ROI_SOUTH_SUMMER_100M (Speyerer et al. 2020), PDS LROC node. Registered to the LOLA grid within one 240 m pixel (correlation 0.884, best match at zero shift).
Site finder Every one of 3,600 precomputed horizons (one per 5.04 km cell from 85°S to the pole, at the brightest pixel of the PGDA average-visibility map) is evaluated over the stay; nothing is sampled at random. Survey sunlit share compared with the PGDA average-visibility value at each point (docs/validation.md).
Not modelled Electrical power; thermal limits; rocks, craters and slopes below the LOLA grid spacing (slope is reported at 10 m and 40 m baselines only); terrain north of 60°S; satellite manoeuvres; navigation (GNSS geometry).
